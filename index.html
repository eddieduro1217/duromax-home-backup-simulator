How sizing works

Running vs. starting watts

Motors (refrigerator, pumps, A/C, furnace blower) need extra power for a second or two when they start. The simulator checks your running total against the generator's running watts, and each start-up against its peak (starting) watts. Start large motors one at a time.

Our recommendation

We recommend a generator whose running watts cover your running load plus a 20% reserve, and whose peak watts cover your running load plus the largest single start-up. Always use the ratings for the fuel you'll actually use: propane and natural gas produce less power than gasoline.

Central A/C

Central A/C is usually the biggest start-up load. Sizes are based on typical single-stage units. For an accurate result, enter the RLA and LRA from your outdoor unit's nameplate. The indoor blower runs with the A/C and is counted separately. A soft starter, like the AirGo, reduces the start-up surge. Your HVAC professional should confirm it's compatible with your unit.

How the generator connects

The simulator connects each generator the way we recommend for home backup: through its 50A outlet (14-50R, up to 12,000 W) or 30A outlet (L14-30R, up to 7,200 W), a generator cord and a power inlet box on the outside of the house. Models without either outlet use extension cords, which can only run 120V appliances. Interlock kit: a slide plate on your breaker panel that holds the main breaker off while the generator breaker is on, so power can never flow back onto the utility lines. Every circuit is available, and you choose what to run. Transfer switch: a small subpanel wired to pre-selected circuits. Up to 7,200 W, use a 6-circuit switch with a 30A inlet. Above 7,200 W, use a 10-circuit switch with a 50A inlet.

Bonded vs. floating neutral

Floating-neutral generators connect to a standard 2-pole transfer switch or interlock as-is. Bonded-neutral generators need the bond removed by a qualified electrician (DuroMax publishes instructions) or a neutral-switching transfer switch.

Safety

Only run a generator outdoors, away from doors, windows and vents. Never plug a generator into a wall outlet (backfeeding). Home connections must be installed by a licensed electrician.
