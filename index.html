4. Breaker panel

How it works

Interlock kit

A slide plate on your breaker panel cover. With it slid up, the generator (backfeed) breaker can be switched on while the main breaker is held off, so generator power can never flow back onto the utility lines. You pick which breakers to run, and can change them anytime.

Transfer switch

A small subpanel an electrician wires to a set of pre-selected circuits. Up to 7,200 W: 6-circuit switch with a 30A inlet. Above 7,200 W: 10-circuit switch with a 50A inlet. A 240V load uses two circuits.

Extension cords

No installation - plug appliances straight into the generator. 240V appliances (well pump, water heater, range, electric dryer, central AC) can't run this way. Put each high-draw appliance on its own cord.

Bonded vs floating neutral

Floating-neutral generators connect to a standard 2-pole transfer switch or interlock as-is. Bonded-neutral generators need the bond removed by a qualified electrician (DuroMax publishes instructions) or a neutral-switching switch. Never run a generator with the bond removed unless it is grounded through the home. On several models, removing the bond disables the GFCI outlets.

Running vs starting watts

Motors (fridge, pumps, AC, furnace blower) need extra power for a second or two when they start. The simulator checks the running total against the generator's running watts (limited by the cord/inlet: 30A = 7,200 W, 50A = 12,000 W), and each start-up against its starting (peak) watts.

Wattages are typical estimates - check each appliance's nameplate. Connecting a generator to home wiring requires an approved transfer switch or interlock kit installed by a licensed electrician per local code. Generator specs from duromaxpower.com, Sept 2026.
